## Consulted primary evidence

- Taka, Kurokawa, Araki & Mikami (2006), DOI 10.1111/j.1550-7408.2006.00091.x, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16677339/ : indexed primary abstract consulted. Usually one vegetative P. caudatum MIC; division fate and disappearance of extra presumptive germline products are separate. Full figures not accessed.
- Nakajima, Ishida & Mikami (2002), DOI 10.1111/j.1550-7408.2002.tb00344.x, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1550-7408.2002.tb00344.x : publisher abstract/authorship retrieved. One postmeiotic nucleus survives at the paroral region; it forms stationary and migratory pronuclei. Reciprocal exchange precedes fusion. Antibody/microinjection experiments support microtubule involvement. Original full figures remain access-limited.
- Yang & Takahashi (1999), DOI 10.1111/j.1550-7408.1999.tb04583.x, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10188260/ : authoritative abstract consulted during the R1.7 specification and indexed recheck. Third postzygotic division yields eight nuclei; positional differentiation must not be confused with immediate degradation. Heat-shocked abnormal cells are not normal teaching anatomy.
- Kimura, Mikami & Endoh (2004), DOI 10.1002/gene.20060, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15354289/ : full authoritative abstract retrieved. Old-MAC fragmentation is not instantaneous DNA degradation; parental fragments can persist across later cell cycles while the new MAC develops. Numerical timing is experiment-specific, not demonstration speed.
- Tucker, Beisson, Roche & Cohen (1980), DOI 10.1242/jcs.44.1.135, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7440651/ : full authoritative abstract retrieved. Primary P. tetraurelia microscopy demonstrates organized intranuclear microtubules and challenges a literal 'amitosis' description. Corrected R1.7 wording to 'traditionally called amitosis'; no claim of absence of microtubules or a featureless cut. Do not transfer its dimensions/kinetics to P. caudatum.

## Further primary sequence evidence

- Mikami (1996), DOI 10.1111/j.1550-7408.1996.tb02471.x, publisher abstract retrieved: the named P. caudatum cycle has two meiotic divisions, one haploid pronuclear mitosis and three synkaryon mitoses. Microsurgery-induced extra divisions are experimental abnormalities, not the default sequence.
- Ishida, Nakajima, Kurokawa & Mikami (1999), Zoological Science 16:915–926, https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/prepareDownload?contentNo=1&itemId=info%3Andljp%2Fpid%2F10862438 : primary scanned PDF retrieved. Actual pages 920–921 / Figures 4–5 were inspected, including legends and staining context. In these observations pair separation followed the first synkaryon division; subsequent postzygotic divisions and four/four nuclear differentiation were distinct. The module must not portray the observation as universal exact timing in every strain. The old oral apparatus is remodeled during meiosis; an oral-facing contact cue must not be described as an unchanged functioning feeding apparatus throughout conjugation. ## Illustration and terminology limits

The whole-cell plate is a horizontal schematic with anterior left, posterior right, a larger hatched MAC and compact MIC. Independently enlarged insets are not additional nuclei. Nuclear location, indentation, cilia density, chromatin marks, chromosome symbols and relative sizes are not calibrated measurements. Chromosome cartoons will use representative homolog/sister symbols, not an invented species karyotype. MAC/MIC are somatic/germline compartments, never male/female nuclei.

Tamil compatibility anchors: பேருட்கரு (MAC), சிற்றுட்கரு (MIC), உட்கரு இருவகைமை, இருமயம் / ஒருமயம், ஒத்த குரோமோசோம்கள், சகோதரி குரோமாட்டிடுகள், குறுக்குத் திசை இருபிளவு, இணைவு. English technical terms remain at introduction. Named Tamil reviewer decision remains PENDING.

## Implemented conjugation controls — assisted review, not human approval

Eight complete numbered English–Tamil subsections accompany sixteen native views. The reference sequence keeps two cells through meiosis, survivor selection, haploid pronuclear mitosis, reciprocal exchange, diploid synkaryon formation, the first postzygotic mitosis, pair separation, the second and third postzygotic mitoses, and nuclear differentiation. Two explicitly later cytokineses raise the illustrated lineage to four and eight cells. The four developing somatic nuclei per exconjugant are distributed across those later divisions; they are not drawn undergoing the MIC chromosome cartoon.

Actual original-paper text/pages were revisited during implementation: Ishida et al., pp.920–921, Fig.5 legend places separation after first synkaryon mitosis; p.925 distinguishes the four still physically present presumptive germline nuclei from their later division fates. The plate retains four at differentiation and early MAC development. Taka's later-cycle selection evidence and Kimura's delayed parental-DNA degradation prevent an instantaneous three-nucleus deletion or immediate old-MAC disappearance. Residual signs after later fissions sit in a separate explanatory inset; no invented per-daughter remnant allocation is asserted.

Partner A/B solid-dot and striped marks follow both migratory pronuclei on distinct native animated tracks. Each stationary nucleus stays in its own cell; received partner origin and mixed synkaryon origin are represented separately in the pure model. These marks are partner-origin teaching conventions, not literal alleles or complete meiotic genotypes. One representative homologous pair demonstrates replication, homolog separation at meiosis I, sister separation at meiosis II and restoration of 2n only at fusion. Recombination is explained in text; actual crossover sites and the species karyotype are not invented.

New Tamil correspondence was reviewed against the English scripts for all eight readings and sixteen stages. நிலை முன்உட்கரு / நகரும் முன்உட்கரு distinguish stationary/migratory roles; பேருட்கரு முதற்கரு introduces anlage; சின்காரியான் is explained as the fused zygotic nucleus.
