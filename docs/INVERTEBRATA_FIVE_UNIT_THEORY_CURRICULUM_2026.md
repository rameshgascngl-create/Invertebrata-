## Common required structure for EACH chapter
1. Learning outcomes (English and standard Tamil).
2. Definition, context and key terminology; distinguish traditional taxonomy from modern classification.
3. Detailed explanatory theory with titled subsections, simple English and parallel accurate Tamil.
4. Structure–function relationships, stepwise physiology and life cycles where applicable.
5. One or more scientifically reviewed, contextual, legible pencil-style raster anatomical figures with captions; never duplicate a plate to fill space. Unreviewed debug assets are NOT approved.
6. Key biological/ecological/medical/economic significance as relevant.
7. Concept summary and glossary, then aligned A5 questions with explanations.
8. Academic, Tamil editorial, image and Android QA gates individually recorded; do not infer acceptance from source presence.

## Unit I — Protozoa and protistan biology
- U1.1 Foundations: animal diversity, levels of organisation, symmetry, body cavities and taxonomy context.
- U1.2 Protozoa: historical grouping, modern protistan diversity, classification up to classes as prescribed; representative examples.
- U1.3 **Paramecium type study**: habit/habitat, morphology, pellicle/cilia, oral apparatus, food vacuoles, digestion, cytoproct, osmoregulation/contractile vacuoles, respiration/excretion, macro-/micronucleus, binary fission, conjugation, ecological significance.
- U1.4 Nutrition and locomotion in protozoans: mechanisms and comparisons.
- U1.5 Parasitic protozoans: Entamoeba, Trypanosoma, Leishmania; transmission, pathogenesis and prevention.
- U1.6 Plasmodium: two-host life cycle, human blood/liver stages, mosquito sexual stages and disease.
- U1.7 Host–parasite relationships, adaptations and ecological/medical importance.

## Unit II — Porifera and Cnidaria
- U2.1 Porifera: general characters, classification and examples.
- U2.2 **Sycon type study**: morphology, body-wall organisation, choanocytes, water-current route, skeleton/spicules, nutrition, respiration, reproduction.
- U2.3 Asconoid, syconoid and leuconoid canal systems: comparative water-flow explanation.
- U2.4 Sponge reproduction and development.
- U2.5 Cnidaria: general characters, classification, cnidocytes, tissue organisation.
- U2.6 **Obelia type study**: colony architecture, hydranths, gonangia, medusa, gametes, planula, metagenesis.
- U2.7 Polymorphism and division of labour in hydrozoans.
- U2.8 Corals and coral reef formation.
- U2.9 Ecological and economic importance, reef threats and conservation.

## Unit III — Platyhelminthes and Nematoda
- U3.1 Platyhelminthes: characters, classification, acoelomate organisation.
- U3.2 **Fasciola hepatica type study**: morphology, tegument, suckers, digestive/excretory/nervous/reproductive systems, full snail–mammal life cycle.
- U3.3 Flatworm parasitic adaptations and representative parasites.
- U3.4 Nematoda (traditional Aschelminthes): characters, classification, pseudocoelom and cuticle.
- U3.5 **Ascaris lumbricoides type study**: sexual dimorphism, body wall, gut, excretory/nervous/reproductive systems, human life cycle.
- U3.6 Nematode parasites, transmission and health significance.
- U3.7 Comparative helminth adaptations and prevention.

## Unit IV — Annelida and Arthropoda
- U4.1 Annelida: general characters, classification, coelom and metamerism.
- U4.2 **Earthworm (identified Metaphire/Pheretima species) type study**: external segmentation, locomotion/setae, alimentary canal, blood vascular system, respiration, nephridia, nervous and reproductive systems, cocoon development, soil ecology. Use species-specific anatomy.
- U4.3 Nereis comparative biology: parapodia, organ systems and epitoky where appropriate.
- U4.4 Arthropoda: characters, classification, segmentation, exoskeleton and moulting.
- U4.5 **Penaeus type study**: morphology, cephalothorax/abdomen, specialised appendages, digestive, respiratory, circulatory, excretory, nervous and reproductive systems.
- U4.6 Crustacean larval forms and development; distinguish species-specific sequences.
- U4.7 Peripatus/Onychophora affinities and evolutionary interpretation.
- U4.8 Applied significance and selected arthropod adaptations.

## Unit V — Mollusca and Echinodermata
- U5.1 Mollusca: general characters, classification, mantle, shell, foot, radula and respiration; note exceptions.
- U5.2 **Pila type study**: habit, external anatomy, shell/operculum, mantle cavity, ctenidium and pulmonary sac, digestive/circulatory/excretory/nervous/reproductive systems, sexual dimorphism.
- U5.3 Gastropod torsion and its developmental/functional consequences.
- U5.4 Foot modifications, cephalopods and molluscan diversity.
- U5.5 Pearl formation and economic importance.
- U5.6 Echinodermata: characters, classification, pentaradial adult and bilateral larval organisation.
- U5.7 **Asterias type study**: external morphology, endoskeleton, water vascular system, tube feet, digestive, nervous and reproductive systems, regeneration.
- U5.8 Echinoderm larvae and developmental significance.
- U5.9 Evolutionary affinities and deuterostome relationships.
- U5.10 Ecological importance and fossil record.
